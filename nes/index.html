Rust NES Workbench

A complete NES in Rust and WebAssembly: 6502 CPU, PPU, APU, controllers, and the six mappers most games use (NROM, MMC1, UxROM, CNROM, MMC3, AxROM). The sample cartridge now runs on an MMC3: its scanline IRQ holds the bottom rows still while the rest scrolls, and CHR bank switching spins the balls. Open your own .nes file to play it; battery saves are kept in this browser. Controls: arrow keys, Z (B), X (A), Shift (Select), Enter (Start), a gamepad, or the buttons under the screen. On a phone, tap Full screen for large controls: in landscape the D-pad and A/B sit either side of the picture. Press Sound on for audio.

    Bars show reads in grey and writes in red. Writes to $8000–$FFFF can't change ROM; they set the mapper's registers. Each frame the sample writes 5 there (4 to re-arm the MMC3 IRQ, 1 to acknowledge it), plus 2 to switch a CHR bank every fourth frame.

    Timing is cycle-accurate: every CPU cycle is one bus access, and the PPU (3 dots) and APU run alongside it, so register reads and writes land on the right dot. Interrupts are polled on an instruction's second-to-last cycle, as on the 6502. Of the three failures, one MMC3 test checks an older chip revision that contradicts the one emulated, and two measure a DMC sample fetch overlapping sprite DMA.
