Rust NES Workbench

A complete NES in Rust and WebAssembly: 6502 CPU, PPU, APU, controllers, and the six mappers most games use (NROM, MMC1, UxROM, CNROM, MMC3, AxROM). The sample cartridge now runs on an MMC3: its scanline IRQ holds the bottom rows still while the rest scrolls, and CHR bank switching spins the balls. Open your own .nes file to play it; battery saves are kept in this browser. Controls: arrow keys, Z (B), X (A), Shift (Select), Enter (Start), a gamepad, or the buttons under the screen. Press Sound on for audio.

    Bars show reads in grey and writes in red. Writes to $8000–$FFFF can't change ROM; they set the mapper's registers. Each frame the sample writes 5 there (4 to re-arm the MMC3 IRQ, 1 to acknowledge it), plus 2 to switch a CHR bank every fourth frame.

    The failures need timing finer than one instruction: the CPU runs a whole instruction, then the PPU and APU catch up. Exact NMI timing, mid-instruction register reads and the MMC3's IRQ timing check that. One MMC3 test checks an older chip revision that contradicts the newer one emulated here.
